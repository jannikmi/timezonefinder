# IDX-4 — decide what a lookup answers on the −180° meridian and the −90° parallel

- **What it is:** no polygon owns a point exactly on the −180° meridian or the −90° parallel, because the kernel gives a boundary point only to a polygon whose interior lies immediately west or south of it, and nothing lies there; `tests/test_point_in_polygon_boundary_convention.py` pins the rule. So both indexes answer such a point with a cell's untested last zone, and `certain_timezone_at` answers `None`. The +180° meridian and the +90° parallel have no such gap: the polygon west or south of them owns every point there, the −180° corner apart.
- **Measured against `master` at f706824 and data 3.2026.4**, on every 0.01° of the −180° meridian (18,001 latitudes) and of the −90° parallel (36,001 longitudes), through `timezone_at`, `timezones_at`, `certain_timezone_at` and `TimezoneFinderL`, on the shipped H3 index and on the chosen tree (`build_grid_tree(…, 384, 4, 1, …)` in `prototypes/latlng_index_bench.py`; the [aligned-index decisions](../../decisions/longitude-latitude-aligned-index-decisions.md) hold its sampled first look). The zone owning each side of the meridian is what `certain_timezone_at` answers at +180° and one unit east of −180°.
  - **The −90° parallel moves only `certain_timezone_at`.** Every option, on both indexes, answers `Antarctica/McMurdo` at every longitude through the other three methods; answering the point one unit north turns `certain_timezone_at`'s `None` into `Antarctica/McMurdo`. A dataset dividing the polar cap would make the fallback index-dependent there too.
  - **The two sides of the meridian disagree at 85.6 % of latitudes:** `Etc/GMT-12` (UTC+12) west against `Etc/GMT+12` (UTC−12) east at 14,911, and Antarctica west against ocean east from −75.2° to −70.1°. Elsewhere one zone owns both sides: Antarctica south of that, `Asia/Anadyr`, `Pacific/Fiji`, `Pacific/Funafuti` and `America/Adak`.
  - **Neither index's fallback is a neutral answer.** H3's agrees with the west side at 84.3 % of latitudes but answers a zone owning neither side at 725 (4.0 %): `Asia/Anadyr` at 225 where both sides are ocean, `Etc/GMT-11`, `Etc/GMT+11` and `Etc/GMT+1` near the pole, and ocean at 151 a land zone owns. The tree's agrees with the east side at 99.0 % and answers ocean at 162 a land zone owns. Keeping the fallback therefore moves 75.0 % of meridian answers when IDX-6 lands, and leaves wrong ones on either index.
  - **`TimezoneFinderL`:** −180° and +180° fall in the same H3 cell at all 18,001 latitudes, so on H3 no option moves an answer of it. On the tree, reading −180° as +180° moves 14.8 % of them to the +180° column's cells; the other options move none against the tree's own fallback.
- **Decision needed:** what a lookup answers for a point on the −180° meridian or the −90° parallel, which no polygon owns. [IDX-6](idx-6-replace-the-h3-shortcut-index-with-the-grid-tree.md)'s reader waits on it, since the tree's prologue is where one of the options lives.
  - **(1) Read −180° as +180°, and −90° as the point one unit north.** Every valid coordinate is then owned by a polygon of the packaged data: one physical point answers one zone whichever spelling it arrives in, by the kernel's own rule that a border point belongs to the polygon west of it; `certain_timezone_at` stops answering `None`, and nothing depends on the index. Moves 15.7 % of H3's meridian answers (2,826: `Etc/GMT+12` to `Etc/GMT-12` north of 63°, ocean to Antarctica from −75.2° to −70.1°, and the spurious `Asia/Anadyr`), after which IDX-6 moves none. Ships on H3, ahead of IDX-6.
  - **(2) Read −180° as the point one unit east, and −90° as one unit north.** Each spelling answers the side it names: `lng=-180` takes UTC−12 like the points just east of it, `lng=180` UTC+12, so the two spellings of one point answer a day apart at 85.6 % of latitudes. Equal to the tree's fallback at 99.0 % of latitudes but exact; moves 74.7 % of H3's meridian answers, on H3 rather than with IDX-6.
  - **(3) Keep the untested fallback on both lines.** No code: answers follow the index, moving 75.0 % of the meridian's with IDX-6 and leaving a zone owning neither side at 1–4 % of latitudes on either index. `certain_timezone_at` keeps answering `None`, and the documentation's exception stands.
  - **Recommendation: (1).** It is the only option under which the answer is a property of the point rather than of the index or the spelling, and it moves the fewest answers. (2) puts one physical point a day apart by spelling and moves almost five times as many; (3) keeps answers wrong under any reading and defers a 75 % change into a format release where it is one line among many. Evidence that callers send −180° meaning the western edge, expecting the zone east of it, would make it (2).
  - **Query-path cost:** a unique-cell query need pay nothing under (1) or (2). H3 already puts −180° and +180° in one cell, and under (1) the tree's clamp of a negative column to the first becomes a wrap to the last, the same branch; what remains is one comparison in the integer conversion ahead of the candidate loop, per ambiguous point — argued, not measured. Rewriting the float in `validate_coordinates` instead is the form to avoid: it measured +51 ns median per scalar call, slower in 25 of 31 alternating rounds, about 3 % of a unique-cell `timezone_at` (C extension, Intel Xeon 2.8 GHz container).
  - **Reversibility:** each option is a few lines in the lookup prologues plus tests and documentation; moving between them later costs the same lines and a second user-visible change of the meridian's answers.
  - **Unpriced:** what callers send at exactly −180° or −90°; and the ambiguous-path comparison, which no instrument here resolves.
- **Documentation that follows the decision:** the pages say that no polygon contains a point on −180° or −90° — the `certain_timezone_at()` and `timezones_at()` notes in `docs/1_usage.rst`, the acceleration-path exception and the `timezones_at()` bullet in `docs/result_stability.rst`, and the certainty-probe paragraph in `docs/alternatives.rst`.
- **Size:** S — under (1) or (2), the rewrite in the integer conversions and the tree's prologue, tests on both lines through all four methods, and the pages above; nothing under (3).
- **Status:** needs the decision above; IDX-6's reader waits on it.
