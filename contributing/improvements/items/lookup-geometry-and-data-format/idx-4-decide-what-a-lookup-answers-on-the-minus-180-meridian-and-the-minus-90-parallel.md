# IDX-4 — answer the −180° meridian as +180° and the −90° parallel as the point one unit north

- **What it is:** no polygon owns a point exactly on the −180° meridian or the −90° parallel, because the kernel gives a boundary point only to a polygon whose interior lies immediately west or south of it, and nothing lies there; `tests/test_point_in_polygon_boundary_convention.py` pins the rule. So both indexes answer such a point with a cell's untested last zone, and `certain_timezone_at` answers `None`. The +180° meridian and the +90° parallel have no such gap: the polygon west or south of them owns every point there, the −180° corner apart.
- **Measured against `master` at f706824 and data 3.2026.4**, on every 0.01° of the −180° meridian (18,001 latitudes) and of the −90° parallel (36,001 longitudes), through `timezone_at`, `timezones_at`, `certain_timezone_at` and `TimezoneFinderL`, on the shipped H3 index and on the chosen tree (`build_grid_tree(…, 384, 4, 1, …)` in `prototypes/latlng_index_bench.py`; the [aligned-index decisions](../../decisions/longitude-latitude-aligned-index-decisions.md) hold its sampled first look). The zone owning each side of the meridian is what `certain_timezone_at` answers at +180° and one unit east of −180°.
  - **The −90° parallel moves only `certain_timezone_at`.** Every option, on both indexes, answers `Antarctica/McMurdo` at every longitude through the other three methods; answering the point one unit north turns `certain_timezone_at`'s `None` into `Antarctica/McMurdo`. A dataset dividing the polar cap would make the fallback index-dependent there too.
  - **The two sides of the meridian disagree at 85.6 % of latitudes:** `Etc/GMT-12` (UTC+12) west against `Etc/GMT+12` (UTC−12) east at 14,911, and Antarctica west against ocean east from −75.0° to −70.0°. Elsewhere one zone owns both sides: Antarctica south of that, `Asia/Anadyr`, `Pacific/Fiji`, `Pacific/Funafuti` and `America/Adak`.
  - **Neither index's fallback is a neutral answer.** H3's agrees with the west side at 84.3 % of latitudes but answers a zone owning neither side at 725 (4.0 %): `Asia/Anadyr` at 225 where both sides are ocean, `Etc/GMT-11`, `Etc/GMT+11` and `Etc/GMT+1` near the pole, and ocean at 151 a land zone owns. The tree's agrees with the east side at 99.0 % and answers a zone owning neither side at 171: ocean at 162 a land zone owns, and `Asia/Anadyr` at 9 where both sides are ocean. Keeping the fallback therefore moves 75.0 % of meridian answers when IDX-6 lands, and leaves wrong ones on either index.
  - **`TimezoneFinderL`:** −180° and +180° fall in the same H3 cell at all 18,001 latitudes, so on H3 no option moves an answer of it. The tree moves them whatever the option, against H3's: 14.8 % reading −180° as +180°, whose column's cells it then uses, and 75.0 % under either other option.
- **Decided 2026-10-12 by the maintainer, on `master` at 5144eb5: option (1) — a lookup reads −180° as +180°, and −90° as the point one unit north.** Every point on the two lines is then owned by a polygon of the packaged data: one physical point answers one zone whichever spelling it arrives in, by the kernel's own rule that a border point belongs to the polygon west of it; `certain_timezone_at` stops answering `None` there, and no answer depends on the index. It moves 15.7 % of H3's meridian answers (2,826: `Etc/GMT+12` to `Etc/GMT-12` north of 63°, ocean to Antarctica from −75.2° to −70.0°, and the spurious `Asia/Anadyr`), after which [IDX-6](idx-6-replace-the-h3-shortcut-index-with-the-grid-tree.md) moves none.
  - **Why:** the only option under which the answer is a property of the point rather than of the index or the spelling, and the one that moves the fewest answers.
  - **Refused: (2) read −180° as the point one unit east**, so each spelling answers the side it names. It puts one physical point a day apart by spelling at 85.6 % of latitudes and moves 74.7 % of H3's meridian answers. Evidence that callers send −180° meaning the western edge, expecting the zone east of it, is what would reopen it.
  - **Refused: (3) keep the untested fallback on both lines.** It keeps answers wrong under any reading at 1–4 % of meridian latitudes on either index, and defers a 75 % change of the meridian's answers into IDX-6's format release, where it is one line among many.
  - **Reversibility:** a few lines in the lookup prologues plus tests and documentation; moving to another option later costs the same lines and a second user-visible change of the meridian's answers.
  - **Unpriced:** what callers send at exactly −180° or −90°; and the ambiguous-path comparison below, which no instrument here resolves.
- **The work, on H3 ahead of IDX-6.** Rewrite the integer coordinates where the lookups convert them for the kernel - `_zone_id_among` (which the batch path shares), `certain_timezone_at` and `timezones_at` in `timezonefinder/timezonefinder.py` - so that x = −180·10⁷ becomes +180·10⁷ and y = −90·10⁷ becomes one unit above it. A unique-cell query needs nothing: H3 already puts −180° and +180° in one cell, so the cell's zone is already +180°'s, and `TimezoneFinderL` answers move on neither line. What it costs is one comparison per ambiguous point - argued, not measured. **Not in `validate_coordinates`:** rewriting the float there measured +51 ns median per scalar call, slower in 25 of 31 alternating rounds, about 3 % of a unique-cell `timezone_at` (C extension, Intel Xeon 2.8 GHz container). Test both lines through all four methods, scalar and batch, against the spelling they now share - `timezone_at(lng=-180, lat=…)` equal to `timezone_at(lng=180, lat=…)`, and `certain_timezone_at` never `None` on either line with the packaged data. It changes public answers, so its fragment goes under `changed/`, not `internal/`.
- **Documentation that follows:** the pages say that no polygon contains a point on −180° or −90° — the `certain_timezone_at()` and `timezones_at()` notes in `docs/1_usage.rst`, the acceleration-path exception and the `timezones_at()` bullet in `docs/result_stability.rst`, and the certainty-probe paragraph in `docs/alternatives.rst`.
- **Size:** S — the rewrite in the integer conversions, the tests on both lines, and the pages above.
- **Status:** open — decided; IDX-6 is sequenced after it, and its tree prologue then wraps a negative column to the last cell instead of clamping it to the first.
