# PERF-4 — the mapped fetch re-acquires the mmap's buffer on every candidate


## Related memory

- [Query performance and shortcut decisions](../../decisions/query-performance-and-shortcut-index-decisions.md)
- [Query performance measurement baseline](../../query-performance-measurement-baseline.md)
- **Location:** `timezonefinder/flatbuf/io/polygons.py`, `read_polygon_array_at`.
- **What it was.** Addressing polygons by `(offset, length)` took the mapped fetch from ~4.9 µs to ~830 ns, against ~60 ns for `in_memory=True`. What remains of that gap is not I/O and not the vtable: `np.frombuffer(self.coord_buf, …)` re-acquires the `mmap` object's buffer on every call. Slicing a **single whole-file `int32` view** instead measures **415 ns against 788 ns** per fetch in isolation.
- **What the in-query measurement actually says, and it is a gain rather than a refusal.** Measured inside `timezone_at`, alternating the two implementations round by round within one process — the only design that does not attribute warm-up to the change, now held by `benchmarks/candidate_comparison.py` and recorded in `docs/benchmarking_methodology.rst` — over the committed fixtures, 2000 points per stratum: **−2.0 % on the ambiguous stratum (12 of 15 rounds) and −0.8 % on a mixed workload (9 of 15, which is a coin flip)**. The absolute per-candidate saving reproduces at ~230–320 ns. The share this entry once claimed, "~3 % of a mixed workload" extrapolated from ~370 ns per fetch in isolation, is still wrong and stays withdrawn — a per-fetch figure is not a workload share. What is left is a real, repeatable ~2 % of an ambiguous query with a 12-of-15 win rate, which is not a coin flip and is not noise; it is simply below what the batch suite can resolve on its own.
- **Refused 2026-08-23 on that being under the noise floor; that refusal is superseded 2026-09-06 by the maintainer.** The [ranking rule](../../improvement-ranking-and-eligibility.md) no longer treats a sub-suite-floor ceiling as a rejection: an item is refused when no instrument shows a gain at all, and this one clears that bar twice over (a repeatable in-query win rate, plus the buffer acquisitions it removes as a count). It composes with the siblings on the same candidate loop — [PERF-2](perf-2-the-candidate-loop-builds-a-zone-id-array-to-read-one-element.md) and [PERF-8](perf-8-the-hole-registry-is-probed-with-an-exception-per-candidate.md) — which is where a stack of ~2 % increments becomes suite-visible. Do not sell this increment on its own as a suite-visible speed-up; re-measure it against whatever tree it lands on.
- **The pinning objection turned out to be the weaker half, and that is the part worth keeping.** A whole-file view is a live export held for the accessor's lifetime, which is the pattern behind the `BufferError` fixed in 8.3.0. `cleanup()` closes before it drops: `close_resource(coord_buf)` runs first and the `delattr` loop over `coord_offsets`/`coord_lengths`/`coord_buf`/`coord_file` runs after it. So a view held as an attribute is **still live when the close is attempted**, `mmap.close()` raises `BufferError`, and `close_resource` swallows it — the mapping would stay open until the accessor is collected, which is exactly the deterministic unmap 8.3.0 restored. That is a fixable ordering, not a prohibition: a held view obliges `cleanup()` to delete it *before* the `close_resource(coord_buf)` call and to name it in that loop. An accessor-lifetime export is therefore **not** in itself a reason to refuse a change here — the reason this one was refused is the measurement. Anything that re-proposes a held view has to clear the noise floor *and* carry that `cleanup()` change; arguing that the mapping stays unpinned is not enough, and today's ordering does not give it for free.
- **Status:** open. Two conditions on the implementation, both from the reasoning above: it carries the `cleanup()` ordering change (delete the held view before `close_resource(coord_buf)`, and name it in the `delattr` loop), and it re-measures its own increment in-query on the tree it lands on rather than quoting the figures above.
- **Last touched:** 2026-09-06 — reopened; the 2026-08-23 refusal was under the noise-floor rule that has since been replaced. The measurement is unchanged, only what the ranking does with it.
