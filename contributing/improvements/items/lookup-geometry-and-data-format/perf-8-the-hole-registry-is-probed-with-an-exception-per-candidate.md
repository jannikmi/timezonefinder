# PERF-8 — the hole check raises and catches a `KeyError` on most candidate polygons

- **Location:** `timezonefinder/timezonefinder.py` — `_iter_hole_ids_of`, reached from `inside_of_polygon` on every candidate that survives the bounding-box test.
- **What it is.** `_iter_hole_ids_of` is a generator function whose body opens with `self.hole_registry[int(boundary_id)]` inside a `try`/`except KeyError`. **92.7 % of the packaged boundary polygons (1,225 of 1,322) have no holes at all**, so the common path builds a generator object, enters it, raises a `KeyError`, catches it and returns — to establish that there is nothing to check. `in_any_polygon` then iterates the exhausted generator and answers `False`.
- **Counts, per ambiguous query, over the committed fixtures.** 1.053 candidates are bounding-box tested and **0.779 reach the hole check**; of those **0.498 are registry misses**, i.e. one raised-and-caught `KeyError` per two ambiguous queries, and 0.779 generator objects created per ambiguous query. On the `on_land` fixture it is 0.180 probes and 0.141 misses per query, on the uniformly random one 0.080 and 0.054. The unique-shortcut stratum never reaches it.
- **Measured per probe, hole-less polygon, `clang` / `in_memory=False`, min of 7 rounds over 500 polygons:** the current form **249 ns** with a numpy id and 221 ns with a Python `int`; a `hole_registry.get(...)` guard feeding `range(first, first + amount)` **59 ns** and **36 ns**. So the exception and the generator are ~190 ns of every hole probe, and the whole probe becomes ~4x cheaper.
- **Measured whole-query, paired against the tree at `0c1a748`, 15 rounds a side, 2,000 fixture points per stratum, both estimators agreeing and 15/15 rounds:** `ambiguous_shortcut` **-4.9 % min / -4.9 % median**, `on_land` **-4.2 / -3.7 %**, `random` **-2.3 / -2.2 %**, `unique_shortcut` -0.3 / -0.2 % (noise — that path never enters the candidate loop). A second run a few minutes later read -4.5 / -4.4 % on the ambiguous stratum.
- **The fix.** Replace the generator plus exception with one `dict.get`, and hand `in_any_polygon` a `range` rather than a generator. `_holes_of_poly` and `get_polygon` share `_iter_hole_ids_of` and want the same thing, so the helper can keep its name and return the `range`. ~10 lines, no data-format change, no behaviour change.
- **Why it is worth taking even though -2.3 % of a random workload is under the noise floor.** It is also the simpler code: a `KeyError` on the majority path is the thing being removed, not a branch being added. It composes with [PERF-9](perf-9-the-candidate-loop-reads-numpy-scalars-where-a-memoryview-yields-an-int.md) and [PERF-2](perf-2-the-candidate-loop-builds-a-zone-id-array-to-read-one-element.md), and the three together measured **-30 % of an ambiguous query and -12 % of a uniformly random workload**, which is above what the benchmark suite can resolve.
- **Sequencing:** take it before or after PERF-9; they touch the same method and whichever lands second has to re-measure its own increment against the tree it finds.
- **Status:** open — free, small, measured.
- **Last touched:** 2026-09-05 — found and measured in the query-flow discovery round.
