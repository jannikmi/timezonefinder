# PERF-2 — the candidate loop builds a whole zone id array to read one element of it

- **Location:** `timezonefinder/timezonefinder.py` — `_zone_id_in_ambiguous_cell`, `_prepare_ambiguous_cell` and `_zone_id_among`.
- **Halved once, and now re-scoped a second time; read this before the history below.** The entry used to cover two numpy calls, the second being `get_last_change_idx` — that half shipped as a stored stop index and is gone. What was left was described as "a numpy fancy-index over a list of a handful of elements, where the per-call overhead dominates whatever is computed", with the fix "narrow the candidates in one pass without the numpy round-trip, ~25 lines". **The 2026-09-05 re-reading is stronger than that: the array does not have to be narrowed, it does not have to be built.** `_zone_id_among` reads `zone_ids` at exactly one index — `zone_ids[i]` on the matched candidate, or `zone_ids[-1]` on the fall-through — so the whole gather over every candidate exists to produce one number. It is a deletion, not a rewrite.
- **Re-measured 2026-09-05, `clang` / `in_memory=False`, min of 7 rounds over 400 real candidate lists (mean length 2.67):** `_zone_ids_of` **564 ns**, unchanged from the 556 ns recorded in 2026-08-23 — the *stage* has not moved. What moved is the denominator: an ambiguous query is ~4.7 µs today against the ~10 µs it was before the frame-of-reference payload, so this is **~12 % of an ambiguous query, not the ~5-6 % this entry claimed**. It is the largest non-geometry stage remaining on that path.
  - The old share was also being read against a ladder rung that was itself ~3x too large, because the profiler binds the public checked `zone_ids_of` rather than the internal one the lookup calls — see [PROF-1](../data-pipeline-and-developer-tooling/prof-1-the-stage-ladder-measures-the-checked-public-accessors.md). Two errors in opposite directions; neither was visible from here.
- **Measured whole-query, paired against the tree at `0c1a748`, 15 rounds a side, 2,000 fixture points per stratum:** `ambiguous_shortcut` **-10.1 % min / -9.9 % median, 14/15 rounds**, `on_land` **-5.8 / -6.1 %, 15/15**, `random` **-2.5 / -1.8 %, 13/15**. The unique-shortcut stratum never enters the loop.
- **The fix.** Drop `zone_ids` from `_zone_id_among` and from what `_prepare_ambiguous_cell` returns, and answer with `int(self.zone_ids[boundary_id])` on the match and `int(self.zone_ids[candidates[-1]])` on the fall-through. Both the scalar and the batch path shrink: the memoised per-cell preparation loses a third of what it holds. No data-format change, no behaviour change, fewer lines than before.
- **What to keep while doing it.** `test_batch_and_scalar_agree_over_every_committed_point` is what makes the deliberate duplication between `_zone_id_in_ambiguous_cell` and `_resolve_ambiguous_cells` safe, and both have to change together or it fails — which is the point of it.
- **Ranked on the measurement now, not on simplicity.** -2.5 % of a random workload is still under the 3-9 % noise floor on its own, so it must not be sold as a speed-up in isolation; taken together with [PERF-8](perf-8-the-hole-registry-is-probed-with-an-exception-per-candidate.md) and the buffer views that have since shipped, the three measured **-30 % of an ambiguous query and -12 % of a uniformly random workload**, which is above it. Independent of both; re-measure this item's own increment against whatever tree it lands on.
- **Status:** open — free, measured, and it removes code.
- **Last touched:** 2026-09-05 — re-scoped and re-measured in the query-flow discovery round; the share it was ranked on had halved in the other direction when the frame-of-reference payload moved the denominator.
