# GH-542 — establish what coordinate precision is worth


## Related memory

- [Geometry, data-format, and validation decisions](../../decisions/geometry-data-format-and-validation-decisions.md)
- **Tracks:** issue #542, opened 2026-08-21 to unblock GH-449; it carries what has to be established and why. GH-449 has since been withdrawn, so read the issue for the precision evidence and not for the encoding it was opened against.
- **Why it exists, and what is left of that reason.** It was opened because GH-449's two candidate encodings differed in whether they spend precision, and ~3 MB separated them — a choice nobody could make without pricing ~1.1 cm. That choice is gone: GH-449 is withdrawn and its successor [FMT-2](fmt-2-a-frame-of-reference-payload-for-the-boundary-coordinates.md) targets 10^-6, which the finding below shows is lossless *by construction*. What remains is the genuinely user-facing half — what resolutions **finer** than the source's own are worth — and nothing downstream waits on it.
- **Value:** moderate. It no longer unblocks anything: as a decision unblocker it has already paid out, and what is left is an accuracy question worth answering on its own terms rather than a gate.
- **An unattended pass can produce half of it, and the half it cannot produce is the deciding one.** The competitor half is now cheap and has a home: `benchmarks/test_comparison.py` already runs this package and `tzfpy` over the same committed fixtures in one process, so the disagreement rate — uniform and, separately, near borders — is an addition to an existing harness rather than the prototype the issue proposed. The user half is not: the "0 of 200,000 changed at 1e-6" figure has to be re-taken **with the shortcut index rebuilt from the quantized geometry** — which a pass may now do, since regenerating the packaged data is no longer out of bounds. What it costs is a regeneration and the review of the binaries it moves, not a permission.
- **Half the question is now answered, and it needed no regeneration at all — the source carries six decimals, not seven.** Read off the committed binaries: across all 15,850,626 packaged coordinate values the last decimal digit is only ever `0` or `9`, never `1`-`8`. That is not a statistical argument — a genuinely seven-decimal source would spread the digit over 0-9 — and the `9`s are `coord2int` truncating toward zero (`133580000` stored as `133579999`) rather than a digit that means anything. So the stored 10^-7 step is exactly 10x finer than upstream's 10^-6, and **quantising to 10^-6 cannot change an answer, by construction**: information that was never there cannot be lost. That explains the "0 of 200,000 changed at 1e-6" figure rather than merely observing it, and retires the need to re-take it. `tests/test_coordinate_precision.py` pins the finding, so a future upstream that does publish a seventh digit fails loudly.
- **What it is worth, priced for the encodings that considered it.** Per-polygon deltas as varints over the packaged boundaries: **60.5 MiB** fixed-width today, **33.7 MiB** at 10^-7, **27.7 MiB** at 10^-6. The redundant decimal is therefore ~6 MiB, **~18 % of the encoded size** — but only once the encoding is variable-width. In the current fixed layout it is worth nothing: `int32` is forced by the *range* (±180° at 10^-6 still needs 29 bits), no reachable precision brings the globe inside `int16` (65,536 steps over 360° is 610 m), and the ray-casting kernel's `int32` arithmetic does not care about magnitudes. **Do not propose relaxing the scale factor as a standalone performance change** — it is only ever a term in the encoding decision.
- **What still needs a regeneration** is the part below 10^-6, where real information starts to go: that is where the user-facing accuracy question actually lives. A pass may now do it — quantize the geometry, rebuild the shortcut index from it, and re-take the changed-answer rate against the committed fixtures. It is *not* needed by FMT-2, which does not go below the source's own resolution; pair it with FMT-2's release only if its answer says a finer scale is worth spending.
- **Status:** open — free, and no longer a gate on anything. It stopped blocking GH-449 on 2026-08-24, when the source-precision finding below answered the encoding half; the entry simply went on saying it did until 2026-08-30. Work is in flight in PR #555 (`improve/542-tzfpy-agreement`).
- **Last touched:** 2026-08-24 — the source-precision half established from the committed data without a regeneration, with the delta+varint sizes at both scales; the remaining deciding question narrowed to resolutions finer than the source's own. Corrected 2026-08-30: the blocking and gating claims were stale from the moment the finding above landed, and FMT-1/FMT-2 record why nothing downstream needs this item.
