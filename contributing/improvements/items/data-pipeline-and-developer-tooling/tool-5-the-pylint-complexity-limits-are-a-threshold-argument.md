# TOOL-5 — the pylint complexity limits are a threshold argument, not a defect list

- **Location:** `pyproject.toml`, `[tool.ruff.lint] select`, which does not contain `PLR`; there is no `[tool.ruff.lint.pylint]` section, so every limit would take ruff's default.
- **Refused 2026-09-06 by the maintainer: `PLR09xx` is not selected, and function size stays a review judgement.** Recorded in the [tooling decisions](../../decisions/benchmarking-tooling-and-dependency-decisions.md) so a later discovery pass does not re-surface the counts as a finding. Reopen only on a register finding that actually traces to a function's length.
- **What it is.** `PLR09xx` prices function complexity against a number. Measured 2026-09-06 at c27b452 on ruff 0.15.22, against ruff's defaults: **15 sites** — `PLR0913` too many arguments 7, `PLR0915` too many statements 5, `PLR0912` too many branches 2, `PLR0911` too many return statements 1.
- **Three facts that decided it, kept because they are what a re-proposal has to answer.**
  - **10 of the 15 are in `scripts/` and `benchmarks/`, not in the shipped package.** Only five sit under `timezonefinder/`: `_data_integrity.py:508` and `:566`, `block_payload.py:279`, `utils_clang.py:96`, `utils_numba.py:200`.
  - **The two widest sites are unsatisfiable, not unfixed.** `utils_clang.py:96` and `utils_numba.py:200` are `pt_in_poly_clang_packed` and `pt_in_poly_packed`, the two point-in-polygon backends, both flagged at 11 arguments — and their flat scalar signatures *are* the cffi and njit calling convention. Bundling them into a struct to satisfy a linter undoes what the signature exists for. A rule needing a permanent suppression on its two worst offenders is not enforcing anything.
  - **The margins are thin everywhere else.** Every `PLR0915` site is 51–56 statements against a limit of 50, `PLR0912` is 14 against 12, `PLR0911` is 7 against 6. Only `PLR0913` is far over.
- **Why a ratchet was refused too.** Setting the limits just above the current tree makes the rule catch new growth only, and the number is read off today's worst function, so it is arbitrary while reading as endorsed. The [TOOL-6](tool-6-the-ruff-version-is-pinned-below-0-16.md) decision taken the same day sharpens this: a pass may now raise the ruff pin unattended, and 0.16 promoted `PLR0917` out of preview, taking `--select PLR09` from **15** sites to **20**. A threshold chosen from today's tree is one an unattended dev-tool bump can silently re-scope.
- **Why adopting ruff's defaults was refused.** It is the only option that shortens anything, and it is the most expensive by an order of magnitude: it would rewrite the data-integrity validators and the converter entry points, which are long because they check many things in one exhaustive pass rather than because they are tangled — and it still cannot succeed on the two backends above.
- **Unpriced, and still unpriced.** Whether any of the 15 functions is actually *hard to change*; nobody has been asked, and the counts are ruff's arithmetic rather than a reading of the code. This is the gap a re-proposal would have to fill, and filling it means naming a function that was hard to change, not recounting the sites.
- **Status:** rejected — complexity limits are not adopted; kept as the argument against re-proposing them on the site counts.
- **Last touched:** 2026-09-06 — refused by the maintainer; the site distribution, the calling-convention sites, and the ratchet's interaction with TOOL-6 recorded as the argument.
