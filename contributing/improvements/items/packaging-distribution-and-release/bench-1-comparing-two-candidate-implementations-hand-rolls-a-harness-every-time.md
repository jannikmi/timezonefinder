# BENCH-1 — comparing two candidate implementations hand-rolls a harness every time


## Related memory

- [Data distribution, packaging, and release decisions](../../decisions/data-distribution-packaging-and-release-decisions.md)
- [Query performance measurement baseline](../../query-performance-measurement-baseline.md)
- **Location:** `benchmarks/`, which has suites for one implementation across commits or machines and none for two candidates within one run; `docs/benchmarking_methodology.rst` carries the designs.
- **What it is.** Deciding between two candidate implementations of a single stage — a data structure, an accessor, a dispatch — is the measurement every remaining performance item needs, and nothing in the repository performs it. Each attempt writes the harness again in `prototypes/` and deletes it with the prototype, so the next one starts from nothing and is free to repeat a design already known to give wrong answers.
- **Why an ad-hoc harness is not good enough:** the designs that flatter the newer candidate are subtle, and all of them have been hit here. Alternating the two paths round by round against running one after the other moved a measured difference from **−13.3 % to −0.3 %** on the unique stratum — the whole thirteen points were the first path warming `validate_coordinates`, `h3.latlng_to_cell` and `zone_name_from_id` for the second. Microbenchmarking the stage in isolation put the shipped dict ~100 ns ahead on a boundary the two candidates do not divide the same way. Iterating a dict's keys in its own order is worth 77 ns against 108 ns on the same lookup, a third of the quantity being compared. `docs/benchmarking_methodology.rst` records all three under *Comparing two implementations of one stage*; a harness that has to be rebuilt is a harness that gets one of them wrong again.
- **Why it is ranked here:** the remaining performance items — GH-364 among them — each have to demonstrate an effect in the low single digits, and none of them can be decided without this. **PERF-4 is the worked example, 2026-08-23**: answering it took a third hand-rolled alternating harness, and what that harness showed — a per-fetch saving that vanished into noise as a workload share — was what rejected the item.
- **What it has to do**, all of which has been hand-rolled at least once already: alternate the order of the two candidates round by round, measure the whole public call rather than the stage, sample the inputs at random rather than in a container's own order, and report two estimators so a difference is believed only when both show it. Medium, and it belongs in `benchmarks/` rather than in `prototypes/` precisely because it will be wanted again.
- **Interleaving base and head inside one CI job is a different idea, and likely infeasible.** Recorded so it is not attempted blind: it needs two versions of the library importable in one process, and `utils.py` binds its backend at import time. This entry is about two candidates in one working tree, which has no such problem.
- **Not a gate.** Whatever is added stays reporting-only until a single-runner noise study says what the residual floor is — the same condition `docs/benchmarking_methodology.rst` already puts on `--fail-on-regression`. A gate that fires on drift is worse than no gate.
