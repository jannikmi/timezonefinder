# GH-501 — the data update's payload-size signal has no calibrated band


## Related memory

- [Data distribution, packaging, and release decisions](../../decisions/data-distribution-packaging-and-release-decisions.md)
- **Tracks:** issue #501, whose comments carry the four-release calibration behind the changed-answer threshold and the design finding that ruled out comparing two packaged datasets.
- **What is left, and only this.** The guardrails on the unattended weekly pipeline have shipped: the download is verified against the digest the GitHub release API publishes per asset (`scripts/upstream_release.py`, recorded in the root `DATA_SOURCE`), and `scripts/data_update_guard.py` re-answers a frozen 10,000-point on-land sample, rewrites the committed baseline the update pull request diffs, and stops the run above a 5 % changed-answer rate. The **payload-size signal ships report-only**: `tests/fixtures/data_update/payload.json` records the boundary and hole payload bytes and the polygon counts on every update, and the guard prints their move. Nothing enforces it.
- **The band is symmetric, and small reductions pass.** Decided against the earlier asymmetric proposal, whose reasoning was wrong: four releases of monotone growth do not make a decrease anomalous — they are consistent with ordinary refinement, and upstream may legitimately drop or simplify boundaries. The gate is a threshold on magnitude in either direction, not a floor of zero.
- **Overruling the asymmetry is what unset the magnitude**, and it is the whole of the remaining work. The four-release calibration on the issue produced a one-sided number and there is no symmetric replacement. What those releases bound is the normal range: boundary payload moved +0.29 %, +0.65 %, +1.47 %, and hole payload −0.07 %, +0.09 %, −0.31 %, so **any band at or below 1.47 % fires on an ordinary refinement** — the failure a blocking gate is most exposed to, since a tripped gate blocks the auto-merge (decided 2026-08-23; *report only* and *block on the two hard signals only* were both refused).
- **Two ways to get the number, and the cheaper one now runs by itself.** Set it the way the changed-answer rate was set — convert each of 2025c, 2026a, 2026b and 2026c from its upstream GeoJSON with the *current* code, so that one reader opens all of them, and measure the payload move between consecutive conversions; that is a download and four conversions, and it is what the entry has always recorded as the method. Or wait: the committed `payload.json` now accumulates the same numbers one release at a time, at no cost. Neither is blocked on anything.
- **Do not guess it.** A blocking gate with a guessed band is the one shape this entry has refused twice, and the refusal survives the changed-answer gate having landed: that threshold is 13x the largest legitimate move measured, and no such headroom exists for payload size.
- **Zone changes must not become a gate**, whatever the size band turns out to be. A rename is a removal plus an addition and cannot be told apart from the data, so gating on zone count fires on a routine event. The observed moves are +4, −3, +2 polygons across the same four releases.
- **Status:** open — the decisions are taken and the signal is recorded on every update; what is missing is a measured magnitude, after which the gate is a comparison in `scripts/data_update_guard.py`.
- **Last touched:** 2026-09-03 — the download guard, the frozen sample, the answer baseline and the 5 % changed-answer gate shipped; the entry was rewritten to hold only the uncalibrated size band.
