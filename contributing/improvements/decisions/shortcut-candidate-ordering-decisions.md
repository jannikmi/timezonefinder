# Shortcut candidate ordering decisions

In what order a shortcut cell's candidate polygons are tested, and why every alternative to the shipped key was refused. Split out of the [query performance and shortcut index decisions](query-performance-and-shortcut-index-decisions.md), which holds the rest of the query path; the resolution the index is built at is its own record again, the [shortcut index resolution decisions](shortcut-index-resolution-decisions.md). Do not re-propose an ordering without new evidence.

- **Re-ordering a shortcut cell's candidate polygons by how much area each overlaps the cell — the whole family built, measured and refused 2026-09-06.** Upstream's own suggestion (issue #301), and the key the register kept an entry for because it "is genuinely the right one". Eight keys were compiled into real binaries, spanning the per-cell blend of the two properties available — a zone's vertex count in the cell and the area it covers. **The family is monotone in the area weight and its best member is what already ships**: ranking zones by `vertices / area^w`, an ambiguous query costs **+4.2 % at w = 0.25, +5.5 % at w = 0.5, +9.7 % at w = 1** (Smith's rule) **and +24.8 % at w = ∞** (pure area), for only 0.6 % to 2.8 % fewer tests. No turning point between, and the reverse direction costs more tests everywhere.
  * **Why every key loses, which is the part to keep.** A count of tests removed is a workload share only when the things counted cost the same, and here they do not — the largest-overlap zone usually has the most vertices, so any probability-led key buys fewer tests by making the first test the expensive one. Today's key is not a probability heuristic: it minimises the cost of a *failed* test, which is what the loop mostly does, and that term dominates every weighting of the other. **Sweeping the weight is the method to copy** — it turns a refusal of one key into a refusal of a family, at one pass over the fixtures per key.
  * **Re-ordering candidates moves answers with no data change** — 2 to 158 of 5,000 ambiguous fixture points by key, at points inside two zones' overlapping upstream polygons. Any future key inherits this, unchecked by anything automatic.
  * **`shapely` is not what refused it.** The maintainer decided on 2026-09-06 that a `shapely`-class dependency is acceptable at conversion time, not at runtime; one in the `data` group costs users nothing. Added there to measure, taken out again.
  * **The subject is closed by construction, not by sampling keys.** The provably optimal ordering was derived and verified against exhaustive enumeration on 31,344 cells: within a zone by `c/p`; across zones by `C/P`, a zone behaving exactly as one job of summed cost and probability; then enumerate which zone takes the free final slot, since designating it also removes its shielding. Worth **0.27 % of a mixed workload**, and no difference on every stratum with both estimators agreeing. Re-run `prototypes/shortcut_ordering_optimum.py` rather than re-deriving it.
  * **Relaxing zone contiguity to the final zone alone is correct and worth nothing.** Only that zone must be a suffix, so the prefix could interleave freely under the per-polygon ratio rule: **0.04 ns per ambiguous query**, the constraint costing anything on **16 of 31,368** cells.
  * **The candidate loop is overhead-bound, which prices every future proposal about it.** The kernel calibrates to **`437.5 ns + 0.511·blocks + 1.133·in-band edges`, R² = 0.9886**, against R² = 0.524 for the vertex count today's key sorts on — so that proxy is stale and still does not matter: 437.5 ns per candidate is fixed, ~1.05 are tested, and the loop is **14.7 % of an ambiguous query**. An *oracle* saves 4.7 % of a mixed workload, the ceiling on this subject. Attack the overhead, not the order.
  * **Re-run it rather than re-deriving it.** `prototypes/shortcut_ordering_optimum.py` holds the model, every ordering, the brute-force check, the calibration and the A/B as cached stages; a new key is one function in its `STRATEGIES` table. The numbers and their derivations are in its `FINDINGS` and in [GH-301](../items/lookup-geometry-and-data-format/gh-301-sort-shortcut-polygons-by-overlap-area.md), retained in *Closed*.
