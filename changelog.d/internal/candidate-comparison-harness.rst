comparing two *candidate* implementations of one stage has a harness instead of being hand-rolled per attempt. ``benchmarks/candidate_comparison.py``'s ``compare_candidates`` takes two named callables that each perform the whole public call for one input, draws a fresh random sample per round and hands the same draw to both, alternates which candidate runs first, and reports the best-round ratio beside the round-level win count - answering ``unresolved`` where the two estimators disagree rather than the one that shows an effect. All four properties are ones a hand-rolled harness got wrong here: a fixed order read 13.3 % where an alternating one read 0.3 %, microbenchmarking the stage in isolation put the shipped structure ~100 ns ahead of a candidate it does not divide the work with, and walking a container in its own order was worth 77 ns against 108 ns on the same lookup. ``docs/benchmarking_methodology.rst`` carries the recorded failures; the harness is reporting-only, like every other measurement here
