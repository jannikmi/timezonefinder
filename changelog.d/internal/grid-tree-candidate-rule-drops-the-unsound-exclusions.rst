The converter's rule for a cell's candidate polygons is tight rather than conservative, and rests on a planar predicate rather than on evidence that cannot establish an exclusion. A cell is an axis-aligned rectangle of the stored integer coordinates, so "can this polygon own one of the cell's points" is a question about two rectangles and a ring, evaluated against the point-in-polygon kernel's own edge convention: `scripts/grid_cells.py` holds it. It never omits a polygon the kernel can place a cell's point inside, and over-includes only one whose interior enters the cell without reaching any of its integer points - a sliver under ~1.1 cm, costing that cell one bounding-box rejection. `tests/grid_cells_test.py` holds the rule to the kernel over randomised polygons, and `scripts/audit_shortcut_candidates.py` probes every stored cell's four corners and four edge midpoints on each compiled dataset.
